Org-Level Analysis · 1 of 4
Organizational Strengths
These strengths appear with enough consistency across department submissions to be considered enterprise-level assets. They represent what the organization has to build from.
01
The Training Department
The most widely cited internal asset across all submissions. Community Living names it explicitly multiple times. ABA, Employment, and QA all reference it as something they build on. The ANCOR Moving Mountains Award, apprenticeship program, competency checkpoints, and Relias auto-enrollment are genuine assets. Currently under-leveraged for cross-department consistency.
02
Collaborative, Mission-Driven Culture
Team cohesion, willingness to learn, and dedication to the mission appear in My Day, CSO, NWMO In-Home, NWMO-CDS, ABA, Intake, and Community Living. It shows up in turnover rates below national averages, staff longevity, and internal promotions. This cultural asset is most at risk when the organization grows faster than its systems.
03
Established External Relationships
Domain-specific but consistent: My Day has SC/guardian relationships; Employment has VR counselor relationships; ABA collaborates cross-departmentally with nursing and employment daily; Recreation has funding agency relationships; Health has provider networks. The organization has built significant relational capital without a coordinated way to leverage it across programs.
04
Financial Stability and CFO Credibility
Community Living cited the CFO and UPM by name as a department strength. Finance references clean audits, a passed Rep Payee review, and an unmodified audit opinion. Fundraising has a track record of grant success. Financial stability is a real competitive advantage given how many peer organizations in this sector are not in the same position.
05
Growth Momentum and Physical Infrastructure
UPM, Community Living, and My Day all reference recent expansion as an active strength: new homes, new program locations, new HUD properties. The Trails development, Latte Life, and the Thrift Store are tangible proof points. The organization has demonstrated it can grow, which creates a credible platform for the next phase provided workforce infrastructure keeps pace.
06
Person-Centered Service Model
Named as a defining strength by CSO, NWMO In-Home, NWMO-CDS, Community Living, and My Day. It is the philosophical foundation of what the organization does and how it differentiates from competitors. It is also the thing most at risk when staffing is thin and reactive operations displace proactive planning.
Org-Level Analysis · 2 of 4
Opportunity Themes
Areas where multiple departments independently identified similar potential. These are strongest when pursued with coordination rather than in parallel silos.
01
Cross-Training and Specialization
Surfaces in CSO, Community Living, ABA, Training, HR, NWMO-CDS, and Intake. Departments see real upside in staff trained across multiple programs but are pursuing it independently. The opportunity is a coordinated organizational effort with HR and Training owning the infrastructure.
02
Technology and Data Platform Modernization
Finance, IT, ABA, QA, Community Living, and CDS all identify modernization as an opportunity. Finance is advancing Planful and Power BI. IT is pursuing a data warehouse and Therap API integrations. ABA needs a data collection platform. Every department is pushing on a piece of this without a unified roadmap connecting them.
03
Community and Institutional Partnerships
Employment, CSO, ABA, Community Living, Recreation, NWMO In-Home, NWMO-CDS, Intake, Marketing, and Fundraising all name external partnerships as a growth lever. Broadly recognized but uncoordinated. There is no shared partnership development function connecting these efforts.
04
Brand Awareness and Marketing Reach
CSO, ABA, NWMO In-Home, NWMO-CDS, Employment, and Health all note that potential clients, referral sources, and staff candidates don't know enough about them or about Life Unlimited broadly. Departments are identifying this gap independently, suggesting the feedback loop between programs and Marketing isn't yet working effectively.
05
Revenue Diversification
Finance, Fundraising, Health, Recreation, CSO, Community Living, and NWMO In-Home all see opportunities to bring in new revenue. These efforts are distributed across departments without a unified revenue strategy. Coordination across these parallel tracks could significantly amplify results.
06
Expanded Service Lines and Geographic Reach
Intake, Community Living, My Day, CDS, Recreation, NWMO In-Home, and Marketing all see growth into new counties, cities, or populations as active opportunities. The breadth of this theme signals organizational readiness, but also the need for a coordinated growth framework that sequences expansion against workforce and property capacity.
07
Occupancy and Utilization Optimization
Intake, Community Living, CDS, and NWMO In-Home are all working toward maximum utilization of existing authorized capacity. This is a financially significant operational efficiency goal that deserves a unified tracking mechanism and clear accountability rather than being managed separately within each program.
08
Guardian and Stakeholder Engagement
Community Living, QA, NWMO In-Home, NWMO-CDS, Marketing, and Fundraising all include goals around improving communication with guardians, families, and service coordinators. This spans multiple pillars and would benefit from a coordinated strategy rather than department-by-department approaches.
Org-Level Analysis · 3 of 4
Challenge Themes
Challenges that appear across multiple departments. Several are systemic — no single department can resolve them independently.
01
Staffing Levels and Turnover
The single most pervasive challenge across the entire submission. Community Living, Employment, ABA, CSO, NWMO In-Home, NWMO-CDS, Health, and Intake all name it explicitly. The specific manifestations vary — BCBA shortage, PCA supply, DSP turnover, open requisitions — but the root issue is consistent: the organization cannot reliably staff to its service model. Nearly every other operational challenge is downstream of this one.
02
Inconsistent Policy Access and Enforcement
My Day, Community Living, Training, and QA all surface this independently. Staff cannot consistently find or access policies, policies are not enforced uniformly across departments, and there is no single source of truth for the handbook or procedures. This creates compliance risk and erodes trust in the management layer across programs.
03
Interdepartmental Communication Breakdowns
Intake, Health, Community Living, CSO, My Day, and Finance all name poor cross-departmental communication as a live challenge. The symptoms are specific: information forwarded rather than shared directly, departments not knowing what others can offer, decisions made without looping in affected teams. This compounds the difficulty of executing any initiative requiring cross-department coordination.
04
Too Many Systems, No Single Source of Truth
Community Living, My Day, CSO, Finance, IT, and QA all describe operating across too many disconnected platforms — Therap, Teams, SharePoint, OneNote, Power BI, and email — with no clear ownership hierarchy. Staff don't know where to look, and data doesn't flow between systems. This fragmentation amplifies every other operational challenge.
05
Low Reimbursement Rates and Budget Constraints
CSO, NWMO In-Home, NWMO-CDS, Health, and Community Living all flag low state reimbursement rates as a structural constraint. This is an external funding environment problem that limits what compensation, staffing, and programming the organization can sustain. Marketing and Fundraising are working on the other side of this through advocacy and revenue diversification, but the gap is real.
06
Reactive Culture and Capacity Overload
CSO names this explicitly. The same pattern appears in Community Living, Training, Intake, and NWMO In-Home. Staff are stretched, priorities compete, and longer-term development work gets displaced by immediate operational demands. This is what makes strategic initiatives stall and is the hardest challenge to address because it feeds on itself.
07
Guardian and Family Engagement Gaps
Community Living, Health, QA, NWMO In-Home, and Intake all note that guardians are hard to reach, inconsistently informed, and sometimes at odds with what individuals want. This is a service quality issue as much as a community engagement issue, and no single department owns the solution.
08
Compensation Competitiveness
Employment, ABA, Community Living, HR, and NWMO In-Home all identify pay as a challenge for both recruitment and retention. This warrants separate attention from the broader staffing theme because compensation is a financial and policy decision that requires leadership-level action department managers cannot take on their own.

Potential Org-Level Goals
Areas where multiple departments are working toward the same destination without a shared framework or owner. These are candidates for organizational-level goals with department-level components underneath.
01
Workforce Pipeline and Career Ladder Development
Nearly every service department is working on some version of a formal pathway from frontline to leadership. HR and Training are building the enabling infrastructure. An organizational goal with defined ownership would allow these efforts to compound rather than run in parallel.
02
Turnover Reduction with Data-Driven Targets
Community Living, Employment, CSO, NWMO In-Home, and HR all name retention as a primary goal. Targets vary across submissions. These could be unified into a single organizational retention goal with a defined threshold and shared measurement methodology.
03
Curriculum and Playbook Completion Across Programs
My Day, Community Living, ABA, CSO, Training, and QA all have goals around formal curriculum or playbooks. Several are already committed to in the strategic plan. The department submissions suggest completion is still in progress in many cases. It is worth surfacing whether existing commitments are on track or need to be re-sequenced.
04
Technology Integration and Data Infrastructure
Finance, IT, QA, Community Living, Health, and CDS all converge here. The org-level goal would be to build a unified data infrastructure that reduces manual process work and enables real-time reporting. Each department solving a piece independently risks creating new fragmentation rather than resolving existing fragmentation.
05
Revenue Diversification and Financial Self-Sustainability
Finance, Fundraising, Health, Recreation, CSO, and Community Living all reference new revenue streams, grant growth, or deficit reduction. The distributed nature of these efforts suggests there is no unified revenue strategy. Several programs are pursuing funding in ways that could be better coordinated with Fundraising and Finance.
06
Geographic and Service Expansion
Intake, Community Living, My Day, CDS, NWMO In-Home, Recreation, and Marketing all have expansion goals. This signals organizational readiness but also the need for a coordinated growth framework that sequences expansion against workforce and property capacity.
07
Occupancy and Utilization Optimization
Intake, Community Living, CDS, and NWMO In-Home are all working toward maximum utilization of existing authorized capacity. This is a financially significant operational goal that deserves a unified tracking mechanism and clear accountability at the organizational level.
08
Guardian and Stakeholder Engagement
Community Living, QA, NWMO In-Home, NWMO-CDS, Marketing, and Fundraising all have goals around improving communication with guardians, families, and SCs. This spans the Disability Rights and Exceptional Services pillars and would benefit from a coordinated strategy rather than department-by-department approaches.
