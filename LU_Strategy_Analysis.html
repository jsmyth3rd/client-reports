Opportunities, Challenges, and Goal Alignment — Department by Department
Life Unlimited  ·  Prepared by Page 3 Consulting  ·  May 2026
This report reviews the strategy session submissions from all 19 Life Unlimited departments. For each department, it summarizes the themes in their identified opportunities, challenges, and goals — then maps each 90-day and 12-month goal to the opportunities and challenges that motivated it. Items with no corresponding goal are called out separately. The final section for each department flags the key gaps worth raising in direct conversation with those teams.

My Day empowers individuals to reach their full potential by providing personalized supports to increase independence, meaningful relationships, and purpose in the community.
Opportunities — themes
The team sees meaningful room to grow around individual-level service quality — better documentation of each person's needs, cross-program collaboration, and expanding community presence through school partnerships and grassroots community initiatives. On the workforce side, Therap proficiency and structured onboarding are identified as levers worth investing in.
Challenges — themes
The two most operationally significant challenges are staffing ratios for individuals with challenging behaviors and fragmented documentation across Therap and other systems. Poor cross-department communication — particularly with pharmacy, CL, and nursing — also surfaces as a friction point that affects service quality daily. Budget visibility and categorization round out the financial challenges.
90-day goals — themes
The 90-day goals are a mix of relationship-building, community presence, and operational controls. The free pantry and community relationship goals are action-oriented and achievable. The petty cash transition is a sensible financial control step. Several goals in this horizon feel more like ongoing commitments than time-bounded 90-day actions.
12-month goals — themes
The 12-month goals show more ambition — a structured volunteer pipeline, specialized Clubhouse training, a curriculum position, and expanded billing hours. The Clubhouse-as-standalone goal is the most strategically significant in this horizon. However, several goals introduce new initiatives (storm shelters, face recognition check-in, annual retreat) that weren't connected to any stated opportunity or challenge.
Key gaps to raise
  • The staffing ratio challenge — too few staff for individuals with challenging behaviors — is the most operationally significant issue named in the sheet and appears in neither goal horizon. This warrants a direct conversation about whether it belongs as a Rock.
  • Therap documentation fragmentation and poor cross-department communication with pharmacy, CL, and nursing are daily friction points with no corresponding goal. These may be systemic issues requiring cross-team coordination beyond My Day's own planning.
  • Six goals introduce initiatives (storm shelters, face recognition check-in, annual retreat, billboard-size parking) that have no stated problem driving them. Before building these into Monday.com, it's worth asking: what gap does each of these solve?

To empower individuals to achieve and maintain their goals through competitive integrated community employment and meaningful community partnerships.
Opportunities — themes
The team's primary opportunities center on pipeline and process: restructuring the post-placement transition so clients don't fall through the cracks, and growing referrals through stronger VR counselor relationships. Staff recruitment and retention is also framed as an opportunity — the team sees stabilizing the workforce as a lever for improving everything else downstream.
Challenges — themes
Staff turnover is the central challenge and it cascades. Inconsistent staffing leads to multiple coaches supporting one client, which creates service gaps during transitions and erodes confidence among clients, guardians, and VR counselors alike. On the advocacy side, the team also flags a practical problem: business development takes time they don't have, and there's no system for tracking established employer relationships.
90-day goals — themes
The 90-day goals are well-grounded and actionable. Standardizing checklists, filling open positions, deploying sub job coaches, and continuing VR relationship-building all respond directly to identified problems. This is one of the cleaner 90-day sets in the analysis — each goal is traceable to a specific challenge.
12-month goals — themes
The 12-month goals extend the 90-day logic well. A formal transition protocol from ESP to Job Coach, deeper VR engagement across regions, and a 3-year tenure target all flow from the team's stated priorities. The one outlier is the Proud LU sticker campaign — a community visibility tactic with no stated problem or opportunity driving it. The Advocacy section overall is underbuilt relative to the team's stated 10-year vision of employers proactively reaching out to LU.
Key gaps to raise
  • The Advocacy section is underbuilt relative to the team's stated 10-year vision (employers proactively reaching out to LU). Two real challenges — time for relationship-building, and no tracking system for employer partners — have no goals. This is worth a focused conversation about what a business development discipline actually looks like in practice.
  • The 3-year tenure goal is an outcome, not an action. It describes where they want to be, not what they'll do differently. A supporting goal around compensation, scheduling, or incentive structure would make this actionable.
  • The Innovation and Growth pillar submitted no content. For a team whose 10-year vision includes regional expansion, that's a notable omission worth surfacing.

To support individuals and stakeholders to overcome current barriers and achieve socially significant outcomes through evidence-based practice and dissemination to our community.
Opportunities — themes
The team's clearest opportunities are in clinical standardization and external growth. Implementing proper behavioral assessments, building a skill acquisition library, and developing cross-department training are all areas where the team sees room to establish ABA as a real infrastructure service across the organization — not just a standalone department.
Challenges — themes
Two challenges dominate the picture and are interrelated: a national BCBA workforce shortage means the team can't hire fast enough, and the BCBAs they do have are spending time on direct intervention instead of supervisory and clinical duties. This creates a ceiling on growth and quality. Beneath that, inconsistent data systems, no internal collection platform, and weak skill acquisition programming are service-quality challenges the team is actively trying to address. The incentive bonus structure — flagged as unattainable and uncompetitive — is a retention risk that sits underneath the workforce challenge but gets no goal response.
90-day goals — themes
The 90-day goals are well-targeted and actionable. Beginning standardized assessments, streamlining training for float DSPs, getting BCBAs in front of program managers, and starting the billing accuracy work with Finance all address real identified problems. The tier model solidification and school district outreach show the team is thinking beyond internal fixes toward system-level change.
12-month goals — themes
The 12-month goals build logically on the 90-day work — the RBT Playbook formalizes what the training streamlining started, the Lead RBT and Lead BCBA Trainer positions address the supervisory bottleneck, and the university partnership takes the graduate pipeline from concept to relationship. The Latte Life and Employment partnership goal is ambitious and interesting but has no problem statement driving it — it reads more as a growth aspiration than a response to a named challenge.
Key gaps to raise
  • The incentive bonus structure challenge is the most significant orphaned item. ABA explicitly flags it as unattainable and uncompetitive with the broader market — in a field with a known national workforce shortage, this is a retention risk. It connects directly to the BCBA shortage challenge but neither goal horizon addresses compensation or incentive redesign.
  • The two workforce challenges (BCBA shortage and BCBAs doing direct work) have partial goal responses — hire more RBTs, create Lead positions — but no goal addresses the structural cause: caseload design and how work is allocated between BCBAs and RBTs. The new positions goal helps but may not move the needle without a parallel caseload restructure.
  • The Latte Life and Employment partnership is an interesting 12-month goal but it appears without context — no opportunity or challenge motivates it. Before it becomes a Rock, it's worth asking what problem it solves and whether the team has capacity to take it on alongside everything else.

Opportunities — themes
The team's clearest opportunities lie in the structural advantages already built into their model: 1:1 ratios, person-centered flexibility, and the ability to serve individuals who fall through the cracks of more structured programs. There is meaningful room to grow by sub-specializing, building out group programming, formalizing curricula, and establishing CSO as a known gap-and-transition resource across the KC IDD service landscape. The team also sees an opportunity to leverage their remote model more intentionally through better documentation tools and cross-training.
Challenges — themes
Visibility is the dominant challenge — the department is not well known inside or outside LU, which limits referrals, community partnerships, and the team's ability to influence how individuals and their natural supports engage with CSO. Beneath that, the remote and dispersed nature of the workforce creates real collaboration and consistency gaps. Financially, low reimbursement rates and individuals declining services put structural pressure on the department that the team is working around rather than through.
90-day goals — themes
The 90-day goals are ambitious and broad — 16 total goals across both horizons is a high volume for a lean, remote team. The most grounded 90-day goals are the visibility work (marketing flier, intake partnership), the group capacity build-out, and the workforce infrastructure updates (OJT, SharePoint, cross-training). The sub-department proposal is strategically significant and appropriate as a 90-day deliverable. Several goals read more like ongoing operational commitments than discrete 90-day actions.
12-month goals — themes
The 12-month goals build well on the 90-day layer — moving from proposal to actual sub-department execution, from occasional community expert involvement to all ISD groups being expert-led, and from curriculum drafts to a product others want access to. The 50% deficit reduction target is directionally right but is built entirely on revenue-side goals; no goal addresses why individuals decline services or why DSPs leave, which are the demand- and retention-side levers that would actually move the number.
Key gaps to raise
  • No mission statement was submitted. For a department with this much thoughtful content elsewhere in the sheet, it is likely an oversight — but worth flagging before this is used in any board-facing materials.
  • The 50% deficit reduction goal is built entirely on revenue-side actions. No goal asks why individuals decline services or addresses DSP turnover as a retention problem. Without demand- and retention-side levers, the target is difficult to achieve through capacity expansion alone.
  • The volume of 90-day goals is high for a lean, remote team — several read as ongoing operational commitments rather than discrete 90-day outcomes. Before these go into Monday.com as Rocks, the team should identify the two or three that would have the most leverage and treat the rest as operational cadence.
  • The fundraising goal appears without context — there is no stated problem, donor relationship, or strategy behind it. Worth asking what kind of fundraising, for what purpose, and whether the team has the capacity to pursue it alongside everything else.

Opportunities — themes
The team's most concrete opportunities are in Jackson County expansion, technology integration — particularly AI and a unified shift app — and building the pipeline from transition-age youth through a bridge program with schools and intake. There are also meaningful opportunities to deepen guardian and family relationships through structured programming, and to convert behavioral services from a cost center into a differentiated service model.
Challenges — themes
Workforce challenges dominate and are deeply interconnected: high turnover with unknown root causes, DSPs feeling undervalued, behavioral homes that staff won't pick up at, schedule structures that offer no middle ground, and supervisors trapped in direct care ratio when they need administrative time. Policy inconsistency and inaccessibility appears in multiple rows as a compounding friction. The financial pressure from overtime, unfilled beds, and low individual activity funding runs underneath all of it.
90-day goals — themes
The 90-day goals reflect the breadth of the submission — there are over 15 distinct items, ranging from strategic (identify homes not generating revenue, AI training rollout, bridge program curriculum) to operational (digital transportation logs, business cards, internship program) to aspirational (edible landscapes). The workforce goals are the most grounded: stress and burnout root cause analysis, role-specific onboarding, and the shift app all respond directly to named challenges. Several goals need to move to a parking lot before this becomes a working plan.
12-month goals — themes
The 12-month goals are similarly broad. The strongest are the career ladder, staff retention incentives, the home-matching program before TMO, the guardian training program, and the Jackson County real estate strategy. The weakest — own transportation service, individual-run open house, apprenticeship programs — appear without any motivating problem. The 90% satisfaction score target is an outcome without a strategy. Before any of this is loaded into Monday.com, the team needs to identify the three to five goals that will have the most leverage and treat the rest as operational cadence or future planning.
Key gaps to raise
  • Community Living submitted 28 rows representing multiple contributors writing independently. The result is closer to a team brainstorm than a department plan. Before any of this is loaded into Monday.com, a prioritization session is needed to identify the three to five goals that will have real leverage — the rest should move to a parking lot.
  • The behavioral homes overtime loop is both a workforce and financial challenge that goes unaddressed. Staff won't pick up at behavioral homes due to skill gaps, which drives overtime costs. A day program for behaviors appears as a 12-month goal but doesn't close the loop on the DSP skill gap that creates the avoidance in the first place.
  • The 80% retention goal and the root cause analysis goal both address turnover, but the 80% target is set before the analysis is complete. The sequencing should be reversed — understand why people are leaving first, then set a retention target based on what's actually fixable.
  • Several goals with no motivating problem — edible landscapes, individual-run open house, own transportation service — should be moved to a parking lot rather than tracked as active goals. Including them in Monday.com alongside serious operational priorities will dilute focus and create noise.
