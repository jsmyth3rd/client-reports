Section 1

Overview & Growth Trends

Markeim has grown meaningfully over the past two and a half years, with revenue rising 18% from 2024 to 2025 and the first five months of 2026 already outpacing the same period in either prior year. The catalog of programs has expanded, enrollment has climbed, and the organization is serving more customers than ever — though new customer acquisition has begun to slow, making retention an increasingly important focus.

Revenue is growing faster than enrollment. Registrations grew 18% from 2024 to 2025, but revenue grew at the same rate — meaning per-registration revenue has held steady around $136. January through April 2026 were the four strongest months in the entire dataset, suggesting the organization is entering a new performance tier.

New customer acquisition is slowing. Markeim attracted 617 first-time customers in 2024, 460 in 2025 — a 25% decline. The 2026 pace of 295 through May is roughly consistent with 2025 annualized, not a continued drop. This pattern is common in growing organizations: the easiest customers to reach come first. The strategic implication is that retention of existing customers becomes increasingly important as the easy-acquisition phase matures.

Section 2

Program Mix & Revenue by Category

Markeim operates across eight distinct program types, ranging from summer camps and adult pottery to wellness classes and kids' arts workshops. Camps and Clay Studio have been the consistent twin engines of revenue. The most striking trend over the past two years is the rise of Kids programming — which nearly doubled in revenue from 2024 to 2025 and continues to grow in 2026.

Kids programs are the fastest-growing segment. Kids Arts & Crafts nearly doubled from $12K (2024) to $22K (2025) and is already at $20K through May 2026. Kids Clay Studio grew from $3.8K to $10.7K to $13.8K — a 267% increase over two years. Together, the two kids program lines now rival Adult Clay Studio in revenue and have surpassed Wellness and Adult Arts & Crafts. This trajectory reflects a deliberate broadening of the program calendar toward family audiences.

Adult Arts & Crafts has pulled back. After generating $14.4K in 2024, it fell to $8.4K in 2025 and $6.5K through May 2026. This category operates as an event-driven workshop business more than a recurring class program, and its decline may reflect a shift in programming priority or audience rather than a structural problem.

But the 13% of customers with four or more visits generate 42% of all revenue, making the retention of that loyal core the most important driver of financial sustainability.

Annual retention is running at about 30%. Of the 617 customers who registered in 2024, 185 (30%) also registered in 2025. Of 2025's 645 active customers, 189 (29.3%) are already showing up in 2026. A consistent ~30% retention means that in any given year, Markeim needs to replace 70% of its customer base with new faces. That's a significant ongoing acquisition burden — and it makes every percentage point of retention improvement financially meaningful.

89 customers have been active all three years. This small group — just 6.5% of the total customer base — represents Markeim's most committed community. They average significantly higher spend and engagement across multiple program types.

Haddonfield concentration is both a strength and a risk. The depth of engagement from 08033 demonstrates that Markeim has genuinely become a pillar of that community — 736 unique customers, $214K in revenue, deep loyalty. But the flip side is that any demographic shift, economic pressure, or competitive entrant in Haddonfield would have an outsized impact on the organization. Geographic diversification is a meaningful long-term strategic consideration.

What each town comes for differs significantly. Haddon Township residents skew heavily toward wellness (Tai Chi, Pilates, Yoga) — Markeim functions more like a fitness studio for them. Cherry Hill is the most balanced market, with meaningful participation across clay, wellness, and kids programs. Pennsauken's registrations are almost entirely clay supplies and firing services — a small cluster of working potters who use Markeim as a supply resource.

Summer Camp Analysis

Summer camp is Markeim's single largest revenue item — $81K across three summers. Revenue has grown each year despite declining family enrollment, driven by rising per-registration prices. A particularly encouraging trend: families are registering significantly earlier each year, reducing last-minute uncertainty and improving planning capacity.

Late registrations have been declining — a real improvement. In 2024, 22 families — nearly 25% of all summer camp registrations — signed up after camp had already started. This creates genuine operational difficulty: staffing, supply planning, and capacity management all depend on knowing enrollment in advance. By 2025, late registrations fell to 10 (14% of total). 2026 data does not yet include the late-registration window, as camp begins late June — but the trend is clearly improving.

2026 projections: strongest summer camp year on record. With 60 registrations and $24,750 secured as of May 20 — roughly one month before camp starts — the trajectory is strong. Based on prior-year registration pace (2024 was ~49% complete by May 20; 2025 was ~73%), 2026 is estimated to be approximately 61% complete. That implies a projected total of 80–100 registrations and $38,000–$42,000 in summer camp revenue — well above either prior year. The 10-week schedule (vs. 8 in prior years) provides meaningful additional upside.

Fewer families returning, more registrations per outreach. Overall family counts have trended down (89 in 2024, 73 in 2025), but per-registration revenue has risen sharply ($306 → $401 → $413 through May 20). Revenue is growing even as the pool of returning families narrows — which means both price increases and the expanded 10-week offering are doing meaningful work. Monitoring enrollment volume alongside revenue is important to detect a ceiling early.

Week 1 is always the most popular
In both 2025 and 2026, the first week of camp draws the most registrations. Families planning summer early claim the first available slot. Marketing emphasis on Week 1 opening is well-placed.
Late summer holds up
Weeks 7–9 (August) in 2025 generated 26 registrations — nearly matching the front half of summer. Late summer camp is a real market, not an afterthought.
Fall Break camp launched successfully
Introduced in 2025, Fall Break camp generated $2,800 in its debut year — a strong start for a new offering with minimal precedent to learn from.
School's Out camp is declining
Revenue from School's Out days fell from $3,465 (2024) to $1,650 (2025). Worth monitoring to understand whether this reflects timing, promotion, or audience shift. Spring 2026 data is partial and not directly comparable.

Section 6

Customer Loyalty by Program Type

Not all programs create the same kind of customer relationship. Open Studio functions like a membership — half of its customers return, and 40% are back year after year. Wellness classes retain about a third of participants annually. At the other end, Adult Arts & Crafts is almost entirely a one-time event business. Understanding these differences informs where to invest in retention efforts and where to accept high churn as a feature of the format.

The pottery ecosystem is Markeim's most valuable long-term relationship. Clay Studio students who progress to Open Studio membership average $426/year and return at 40%+ annually — nearly three times the overall retention rate. Ten percent of Clay Studio customers make this upgrade. The pathway is real, documented, and worth cultivating deliberately through programming, community-building, and explicit invitation to move up.

Camps are the best front door for family engagement. Twenty-five percent of camp families also sign up for Kids Arts & Crafts, and 11% add Kids Clay Studio. No other entry point converts to cross-program participation at that rate. A family that discovers Markeim through summer camp and then enrolls in kids classes is worth significantly more than their camp fee alone — and the data shows that a quarter of camp families make exactly that journey.

Kids Clay Studio is a growth story in progress. Year-over-year retention improved from 15.8% to 21.4% — a 35% improvement in a single year. As the program matures and families establish routines, this cohort is on track to develop the same deep loyalty the adult clay program has built over time.

Open Studio → Clay Studio: the core pipeline
52% of Open Studio customers also participate in Clay Studio. This is the clearest progression path in the organization: students start in Clay Studio, deepen their practice, and graduate into the Open Studio membership model. Nurturing this pipeline is one of the highest-value things Markeim can do.
Wellness customers are loyal but siloed
The wellness audience retains at ~33% annually but almost never crosses into art programming. They are a distinct audience who come to Markeim for fitness, not arts. Marketing and programming for this group likely needs to be distinct from the art-focused audience.
Adult Arts & Crafts: event business, not class program
With a 10% repeat rate and under 7% annual retention, this category doesn't behave like a class program. It's a workshop/event series. That's a perfectly viable model — but it requires constant fresh programming to sustain revenue, and shouldn't be expected to build a returning customer base.
Kids Clay improving steadily
Retention jumped from 15.8% to 21.4% year-over-year. With 30% of its customers also doing Kids Arts, this cohort is becoming the kids' equivalent of the adult clay ecosystem — a deeply engaged, multi-program family relationship.
